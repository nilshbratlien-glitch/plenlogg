About Turfkeep

Turfkeep turns what you know about your lawn into a feeding plan for the season, then keeps that plan honest against what you actually do.

Simple mode

This is the default, and for most lawns it is all you need. You give it the size of your lawn, roughly how good you want it to look, where in the country you are, and which bag of granular fertiliser you have. It gives you back a handful of dates and, for each one, how much to spread.

The plan is not evenly spaced. Grass uses nitrogen fastest around midsummer and barely at all at either end of the season, so the doses follow that shape — light in spring, largest around June and July, tapering off before winter. Feeding hard late is the single easiest way to damage a lawn, so the app holds the autumn total down whatever else you ask of it.

Log your feeds and mows as you go. If you feed early, late, or not at all, the remaining dates adjust so the season still lands on target. Skip one deliberately and it redistributes; miss one and it tells you.

Advanced mode

Turn Advanced on in Settings and everything the model actually tracks becomes visible and editable:

Liquid and combination feeding. Spray dissolved fertiliser instead of, or alongside, granular. The app works out water volumes, tank fills and mixing strength, and splits any pass that would exceed what a leaf can absorb at once.

Nitrogen in the open. Doses shown in g N/m² rather than kilograms of product, plus the season target, the autumn ceiling, and where you stand against both.

Soil, clippings and slow-release. Sandy soil holds less nitrogen; collecting clippings removes what would otherwise return, raising the requirement by roughly a third; controlled-release granular allows fewer, larger rounds.

Iron and weed control. An iron programme for colour, moss or nutrition, with the legal line between feeding and moss control marked. A Plenrens calculator with timing guidance.

Nutrient balance. Whether your bag actually supplies phosphorus and potassium in the proportions grass takes them up.

Where the numbers come from

Nitrogen rates, the 100 : 14 : 65 N : P : K ratio, species factors and the seasonal curve come from Precision Fertilisation — From theory to practice (STERF, 2021 update) by Ericsson, Blombäck and Kvalbein, revised by Hesselsøe, Aamlid and Borchert at NIBIO Landvik. The curve is calibrated so a maximum-input southern lawn reproduces their three published rates: a 0.30 g N/m² starting dose, a 0.75 peak, and 0.40 on 1 September.

The autumn ceiling comes from STERF's autumn fertilisation trials. Beyond about 2.8 g N/m² after 1 September, nitrogen leaching climbed toward half of what was applied, creeping bentgrass lost roughly 10 °C of freezing tolerance, and snow mould increased four to five fold.

Two things that are mine, not STERF's: their figures are for golf greens, so this app scales them down for a garden lawn — higher cut, clippings usually left. And the spreader warning uses a practical floor of about 5 g of product per m², below which most consumer broadcast spreaders cannot distribute evenly. STERF makes the equipment point but does not publish that number.
